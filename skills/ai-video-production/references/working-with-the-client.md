# Working with the client

How the person who approves the film is kept able to approve it: what reaches him, in what form, in what order. Learned from a client who reads the chat on his phone, has an ear and an eye you do not, and corrects fast.

## One thing at a time

Ask one question, get one decision, then the next. "Explain to me what you need from me, and I'll do it for you." A list of nine open items is for a ledger; the conversation carries one.

## The script is his

- Write the first draft the way he talks (`principles.md`, "The script") and give it to him as a plain file: a scene per heading, "Picture", "She says", "Then we see", the lines on the call or the screen, the optional pieces at the bottom, and a "Your notes" space. Put a copy in his downloads folder with a plain name and tell him he can also open it in the editor.
- Not an online document on an account he may lose; he lost one when his account changed.
- When he has edited it, diff his version against yours line by line, write what each change teaches, and tell him in a few lines what you learned. He asked for exactly that: "see the difference between what I wrote and what you did, so you can get my style."
- A line he wrote that the product cannot yet make true: build it as he wrote it, put the product change on the backlog as top priority with his words and the why, and tell him once.

## Voices are his ear

- The agent cannot hear. Every voice, every pronunciation, the music: his ear, from numbered samples.
- The samples go on one page he can open online and play (a small HTML page with the audio embedded as data URIs, one row per candidate, a play button, the library's name as a label only), with each list titled by the person in the film: "The elderly woman, one to eight", "The elderly man, her husband, one to six". He asked for that after a page labelled with voice names meant nothing to him.
- If a page needs an account he may not have, give him the files in his downloads folder with plain names as well.
- A pronunciation question he cannot settle from a transcript gets short samples: the name alone in each voice, the line with and without the accent, numbered.

## One piece at a time, raw

For a new film: generate one shot, look at it yourself frame by frame, show it to him raw with nothing on top, take his verdict, carry it to the next shot. He said it twice: "so we don't generate the entire video for nothing" and "I hope you did it in very small pieces so we can actually fix one piece at a time." The whole film built in one day had to be rebuilt once his structural notes came.

## What reaches him, and how

- Previews under the chat's size limit (about 30 MB; a 720p encode of a two-minute film) in the chat; the master in his downloads folder with a plain name (`film-v3-1080p.mp4`); never a path he has to find.
- He will say "the resolution is low" about a preview: say up front that the preview is small for the chat and where the master is.
- Before he sees a render: your own frame grid, your own check of every new element, the talking shots watched at speed. He finds what you missed, and he should find little.
- When something breaks on the machine (a render starved by other work, a venue down), say what happened, what it means for him, and what you changed so it does not happen again, in three sentences.
- When he asks "where are the files": the honest answer first, then the way back.

## His notes

Each round of notes is a new version; nothing is overwritten. Structural notes change the script; a voice note changes one voice; a words-on-screen note is free. Tell him which of his notes cost money and which are free.

## The product's own decisions

A film about a product makes product decisions visible: what the product says on a call, whether it announces itself, what it does after a robocall. When he decides one in the film conversation, write it on the product's backlog with his words, the change, the why and the priority, tell the people building the product, and get back to the film. He set the seat: "Your job is to produce the videos. Anything that needs to be changed in the system, you need to put on the main ledger."

## Closing a session

Go through everything that was done; everything still open becomes one section of the backlog he can read in one sitting: what is his (a word, a look, a listen), what must be true before release, where every file is, what was spent. Commit and push the documents and scripts. Say where the footage is and that it exists only there.
